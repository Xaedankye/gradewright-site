How to drop the lowest quiz score in a weighted grade

Drop the lowest score inside one category before you average that category, and before you apply the category's weight. Dropping at the category level is what keeps the weighting correct — the dropped score never reaches the final average.

A worked example

One student, three categories: homework 20%, quizzes 30% (drop the lowest quiz), exams 50%. Quiz scores are 70, 85 and 95.

CategoryWeightScoresCategory average
Homework20%90, 80, 10090.00
Quizzes30%70, 85, 95 (drop 70)90.00
Exams50%88/100, 76/10082.00

Without the drop, the quiz average is (70 + 85 + 95) ÷ 3 = 83.33, and the final grade is:

0.20 × 90.00 + 0.30 × 83.33 + 0.50 × 82.00 = 84.00

Dropping the 70 leaves (85 + 95) ÷ 2 = 90.00, and the final becomes:

0.20 × 90.00 + 0.30 × 90.00 + 0.50 × 82.00 = 86.00

One dropped quiz moves the final by 2.00 points.

The formula

For quiz scores in B2:B4, the average of all but the lowest is:

# average without the single lowest score
=IFERROR((SUM(B2:B4) - MIN(B2:B4)) / (COUNT(B2:B4) - 1), "")

The IFERROR keeps the cell clean while the row is still empty. To drop the lowest two, subtract SMALL(B2:B4,1)+SMALL(B2:B4,2) and divide by COUNT(...)-2 — the pattern extends, but it gets unreadable quickly, which is the real cost of doing this by formula.

Three ways this goes wrong

1. Dropping after you weight

If you average the quizzes with the 70, weight the result, and only then try to "drop" something, there's nothing to drop — the low score is already baked into every student's average. The drop has to happen inside the category, first.

2. Dropping too much

(SUM - MIN) / (COUNT - 1) on a single score divides by zero. Guard it, and decide what "drop one" means when a student has only one or two quizzes — most teachers cap it at "never drop below one remaining score".

3. Dropping across categories

Dropping "the lowest score overall" is a different rule from dropping the lowest score per category. A student's worst exam might be a 70 while their worst quiz is a 95; dropping the overall lowest favours a different student than the per-category rule you actually intended.

When a spreadsheet formula is the better answer. For one class with a fixed quiz range and a single drop rule, the formula above is fine.
