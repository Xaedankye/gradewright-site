Why teachers pick Gradewright

Gradewright asks for the current spreadsheet only — not your Drive, not your Classroom. Names and scores are read and written in your own Google account. The only thing that reaches our server is a random install ID.

No Classroom, no super-admin

It works on a sheet you typed or pasted yourself. There's nothing for your school's IT department to enable, and no roster sync to ask for.

$12 once, not per month

One payment per Google account unlocks unlimited classes, PDF export, custom letter bands and teacher comments. Free stays free for your first class.

Three steps, one sidebar

No import step, no template to replace. Your sheet stays your sheet; Gradewright adds one column and reads the rest.

  1. Point it at your columns

    It finds the Name column and the score columns, tells you what it found ("28 students in column A, 6 score columns B–G") and lets you correct it.

  2. Set your weights

    Homework 20, quizzes 30, exams 50 — or 2/3/5, or 20/30/45. Weights don't have to total 100; it renormalizes and shows you the weights it actually used.

  3. Compute, then hand back cards

    One grade column, written in place (running it again updates the same column). Then a one-page report card per student — on screen free, as PDF on Pro.

The part most sheets get wrong: weights that don't total 100

A teacher sets 20 / 30 / 45 by mistake. A spreadsheet formula that assumes 100 quietly produces a final grade that's too low. Gradewright divides by what you actually entered and tells you what it used.

Questions teachers actually ask

Do I need Google Classroom?

No. Gradewright works on a sheet you keep yourself. There is no roster sync and no Classroom permission, which is also why there's nothing for a school admin to approve.

Where do my students' grades go?

Nowhere. Names and scores stay in your Google Sheet, in your Google account. The add-on talks to our licence server only to start a checkout and to check a licence key, and those requests carry a random install ID — never a name, a score, a sheet ID or an email address.

What's the difference between blank, excused and zero?

A blank cell is work that hasn't been scored: it's left out of the average. The word excused is also left out, but the report card can show it was excused rather than missing. A 0 is a zero and it counts — which is the whole point, and the reason one careless empty cell versus a typed 0 can move a homework average by 18 points.

My weights add up to 95. Is that broken?

No. It divides each weight by the total you entered (so 20/30/45 becomes 21.05 / 31.58 / 47.37%) and shows you the numbers it used. Nothing is silently rejected.

Can I use it for five classes?

The free version covers one spreadsheet. Pro covers every class you teach, for one $12 payment on your Google account.

Can I move my free class to a different spreadsheet?

Yes — the sidebar offers to move it, and says plainly that the previous spreadsheet stops computing until you switch back or unlock Pro. Your grades and weights there are never deleted.

I changed schools and lost the licence. Now what?

The licence is tied to the install, not to your name. Email support@foxlayne.ai with your Stripe receipt and it gets moved.

Is this FERPA compliant?

We don't claim compliance with anything, and you should be suspicious of a $12 add-on that does. What we can state precisely: the add-on requests the current spreadsheet only, and no student data is transmitted anywhere.
