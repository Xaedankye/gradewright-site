What to do when your weights don't add up to 100

Divide each weight by the sum of the weights you actually entered, so 20 / 30 / 45 becomes 21.05 / 31.58 / 47.37, then multiply by the category averages. Dividing by 100 only works when your weights really do total 100.

The mistake, and the fix, in one example

You meant homework 20, quizzes 30, exams 45 — but 20 + 30 + 45 is 95, not 100. A student has homework 90.00, quizzes 83.33 and exams 82.00.

CategoryAverageYou enteredEffective weight
Homework90.002020 ÷ 95 = 21.05%
Quizzes83.333030 ÷ 95 = 31.58%
Exams82.004545 ÷ 95 = 47.37%
Total95100%

The correct final, dividing by 95 (the quiz average of 83.33 is 250 ÷ 3, so the second term is exact):

(20 × 90.00 + 30 × (250 ÷ 3) + 45 × 82.00) ÷ 95
= (1800 + 2500 + 3690) ÷ 95
= 7990 ÷ 95
= 84.11

The wrong final, assuming the weights already sum to 100:

0.20 × 90.00 + 0.30 × 83.33 + 0.45 × 82.00
= 79.90

Same scores, same weights — 4.21 points apart. The 79.90 number isn't just low, it's meaningless: it only accounts for 95% of the student's grade and silently drops the last 5%.

The formula

# weights in B1:B3, category averages in C1:C3
=SUMPRODUCT(B1:B3, C1:C3) / SUM(B1:B3)

SUMPRODUCT multiplies each weight by its average and adds them; SUM(B1:B3) is the 95 you actually entered. This is the entire fix — one denominator.

Three ways this goes wrong

1. Dividing by a hard-coded 100

The most common version: =(B1*C1 + B2*C2 + B3*C3)/100. The /100 is the bug. Every grade comes out too low, and because the error is the same on every student, nobody notices until a parent does the arithmetic.

2. Weights that change but the denominator doesn't

You edit one category from 45 to 50, so the total becomes 100 again — but only for students whose columns you remembered to update. The formula that divides by SUM(B1:B3) keeps working through the edit; the one that divides by a typed 100 does not.

3. One student is missing a whole category

If a student has no exam scores yet, the exam weight still has to go somewhere. The right move is usually to renormalize over the categories that do have scores (that student's homework and quizzes become 40% and 60%). The exception is when the missing work isn't excused — then score it as a 0 instead of letting the weights redistribute. See excused vs blank vs zero.
