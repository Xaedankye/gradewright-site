How to calculate weighted grades in Google Sheets

Average each category first, then multiply each category average by its weight and divide by the total weight. Doing it in that order is what makes blanks, points-based exams and weights that don't total 100 behave correctly.

A worked example you can follow in your own sheet

One student, three categories: homework 20%, quizzes 30%, exams 50%. Exams are recorded as points earned out of points possible, which is how most teachers mark them.

CategoryWeightScoresCategory average
Homework20%90, 80, 10090.00
Quizzes30%70, 85, 9583.33
Exams50%88/100, 76/10082.00
Final100%84.00 → B

The arithmetic, with nothing hidden:

90.00 × 20%  = 18.00
83.33 × 30%  = 25.00
82.00 × 50%  = 41.00
                -----
final         = 84.00  →  B (90+ = A, 80+ = B)

The formula, in three parts

Put the three category averages in helper cells to the right of the data (columns N, O and P below). It's the one genuinely awkward part of doing this by hand; everything else is short.

# N2 — homework average (AVERAGE already skips empty cells)
=IFERROR(AVERAGE(B2:D2),"")

# O2 — quiz average
=IFERROR(AVERAGE(E2:G2),"")

# P2 — exam average from points: total earned ÷ total possible
=IFERROR(100*SUM(H2:I2)/SUM(J2:K2),"")

# L2 — weighted final, divided by the total weight you actually entered
=IFERROR(ROUND((N2*20 + O2*30 + P2*50)/SUM(20,30,50),2),"")

# M2 — letter band
=IFS(L2>=90,"A",L2>=80,"B",L2>=70,"C",L2>=60,"D",L2>=0,"F")

Three ways this goes wrong

1. Averaging the exams instead of totalling the points

AVERAGE() on exam scores only works while every exam is out of the same total. Add one 50-point exam next to a 100-point exam and it breaks: AVERAGE(42,76) returns 59.00, while the correct points total is 100×(42+76)/(50+100) = 78.67. That's a 19.67-point error on a student's exam average, and it arrives silently.

2. Empty cells that behave like zeros — or don't

AVERAGE() skips empty cells, so a student missing one homework gets an average of what's there. That's usually what you want, but it means an empty cell and a typed 0 are graded differently: four scores of 90 with one blank average 90.00, and the same four with a 0 average 72.00. Decide which one you mean before you fill the row, because the formula can't ask you.

3. Weights that don't total 100

If your columns say 20 / 30 / 45, dividing by 100 understates every grade. Divide by the sum of what you entered instead — 20+30+45 = 95 — and the same scores give 84.11 rather than 79.90. Same data, 4.21 points apart. Full explanation of the normalization case.

Why total points and weighted categories disagree. The same scores above come out at 84.00 weighted and 85.50 by total points (684 earned out of 800 possible). Neither is more correct — they answer different questions about what a missed homework is worth. Which one is your sheet doing?
